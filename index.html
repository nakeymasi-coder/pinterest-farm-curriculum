Connect Muse to Pinterest + Meet Your Farm

Lesson 1Start here — do this first✓ Complete
Why This Matters

Everything in this farm runs on one connection — and it is not a button. Pinterest has no connector button inside Muse. The connection is Muse's built-in browser: Muse opens Pinterest in a live browser right inside your chat, you sign in yourself, and from then on Muse can audit, clean up, and schedule your Pinterest while you watch. Connect first, and the whole farm can work. Skip it, and nothing can touch your account.

Follow Along
  • Open your farm's Home tab and find the card "Connect Muse to Pinterest — Do This First" — it sits right above the Setup Agent
  • Copy the starter prompt out of that card, word for word — here it is again, exactly as it lives in your farm:
    Copy-ready starter promptAct as my Pinterest marketing strategist. Open Pinterest in your browser — I will sign in myself when you show me the login page. Once I say I'm in, audit my profile, my boards, my settings and my analytics, and tell me what needs fixing BEFORE you change anything. My goal is outbound clicks and sales.
  • Paste it into a new Muse chat and send it. Muse opens Pinterest in a browser panel inside your chat
  • When the login page shows, tap the browser panel, take control, and sign in yourself — your password and any code go only into Pinterest's own login page, never into the chat
  • Hand control back and say "I'm in." Muse confirms it can see your account — then it audits before it changes anything
Your Turn — Build It
  • Paste the starter prompt into your own Muse and sign in yourself in the browser panel
  • Say "I'm in" and let Muse run your first audit — profile, boards, settings, analytics
  • Read the audit list. Nothing gets fixed until you say yes — that order never changes
  • Tour your farm while you're here: Home, Pin Intake Form, Character Vault, Solo Agent Forms, Queue, Run in Chat
Remember: your password and your codes never go in chat — not for Pinterest, not for anything. You sign in inside the browser; Muse does the work after.
Tap when you're signed in, your audit is in front of you, and you've toured the farm.

Start From Zero — Set Up Pinterest the Right Way

Lesson 2✓ Complete
Why This Matters

A farm fed into a messy account wastes every Pin. Your farm carries a built-in coach — Agent 6, the Pinterest Setup Agent — whose 7-step "Start From Zero" checklist sets a brand-new or messy account up the right way, in order, before the first real run. Do the setup once and every Pin after it lands on solid ground.

Follow Along
  • On the farm's Home tab, open the Pinterest Setup Agent card — tap a step to open its plain-language how-to, do it, then check it off. The card remembers your checkmarks
  • Step 1: create the account and switch it to a free Business account
  • Step 2: build a profile that sells — clear photo or logo, a keyword-rich bio saying who you help and what you post, and your shop or community link
  • Step 3: claim your website, so your content is credited to you everywhere it travels
  • Step 4: create your first 5 boards with keyword-rich names a buyer would actually search
  • Step 5: turn on Rich Pins and find your Analytics — outbound clicks are the scoreboard from day one
  • Step 6: post your first Pin the right way — strong vertical visual, keyword title, description, board, and a destination link
  • Step 7: set your weekly rhythm — batch and schedule ahead instead of posting randomly
Your Turn — Build It
  • Work the checklist top to bottom until it reads 7 of 7 — hear the farm tell you: "You're set up — now go feed the farm"
  • Name every board like a search phrase, not a diary entry — buyers search "good morning affirmations," not "my faves"
  • Post that first Pin with all five pieces: visual, title, description, board, link
Remember: setup is not busywork. A claimed website, keyword boards, and Analytics turned on are what let Pins turn into clicks — and clicks into sales.
Tap when your Setup Agent reads 7 of 7 and your first Pin is posted the right way.

Lock Your Character in the Vault

Lesson 3✓ Complete
Why This Matters

Your audience falls in love with a person, not a random face in every Pin. The Character Vault locks your character with two reference photos, so she is recognizably herself in every image and every video — same face, same body, same energy. The reference photos ARE the lock. No lock, no consistency — and consistency is what turns scrollers into followers.

Follow Along
  • Open the Character Vault and add a character — a letter and a name, plus a voice description
  • Slot 1 — Headshot: one photo, 9:16. This becomes Reference One in every build
  • Slot 2 — Body Reference Sheet: one single 9:16 photo — not separate photos — with front, three-quarter, left profile, and right side views of the same person, same outfit, same background, all on that one photo
  • She is NOT locked until BOTH slots are filled — the card says so, and the farm means it
  • Uploads: JPG or PNG, as you saved them. A webp gets rejected with a clear message: "JPG or PNG please"
Your Turn — Build It
  • Fill Slot 1 with your character's best 9:16 headshot
  • Build or upload her Slot 2 single-sheet body reference — four views, one photo
  • Confirm the farm calls her locked before you run a single job with her
Remember: your character stays exactly as her reference photos — same face, same body. Plus-size stays plus-size, never slimmed down, and nobody gets swapped mid-run.
Tap when both slots are filled and your character shows as locked.

Your First Full Farm Run

Lesson 4The heart of this curriculum✓ Complete
Why This Matters

A farm run is the whole pipeline, start to finish: five agents working one job in order, and a finished Pin package landing in chat for your approval. Learn this one flow and you can produce a day of Pins — or a week — without ever starting from a blank screen again.

SCOUT→LOCK THE LOOK→IMAGE→STORY + VIDEO→PIN COPY→APPROVE→SCHEDULE
Follow Along
  • Play the Farm Demo at the top of this page and follow one day of Pins through the pipeline, Scout to Schedule
  • On the Pin Intake Form, walk the fields in order: locked character → Mode → Product Presentation → Topic → Pin format → product → reference link → notes → destination link
  • The 7 Modes are your 6 proven pillars plus one: Good Morning Affirmations · Cozy Glam Character Looks · Typography & Word Art · Video Pins · Room & Studio Decor Scenes · Creator Business Tips · and Product Spotlight for interactive Pins. A mode is a setting — it changes the search, the story, the shots, and the copy
  • Product Presentation sits right after Mode — one single choice: None, Wearing, Holding, Using, and five more ways to present it. Wearing and holding never mix in the same shot
  • Submit, and your run lands in the Queue. Tap Run in Chat on it — that sets it to working and opens its Run in Chat card. Tap Copy Prompt and paste it into chat with Muse
  • Watch the agents work in order: Agent 1 Scout reads Pinterest in guest view (keywords, top boards, trends worked 45 days ahead) · Agent 2 Image locks the outfit on your character in chat and builds the reference sheet · Agent 3 Story writes hook, shots, and script — scene action only, never a description of her · Agent 4 Video builds the video Pin · Agent 5 Pin Copy finishes the package
Your Turn — Build It
  • Fill the intake form for one real Pin — pick your strongest pillar and give the Pin a destination link before you submit
  • Queue it, Run in Chat, paste the copied prompt, and follow the package as it comes back
  • Check the finished package against the standing order: keyword title, board, destination link — all three, or it is not done

New: Scene Sets — one product, four Pins

Your Image Agent also runs the Scene Set workflow. Flip Scene Set on in the Pin Intake form (or in the Image Agent's solo form), give the run one person reference and one product reference, and instead of a single image you get a 4-shot creator set — four standalone images, each on its own canvas, never a grid:

  • Hero — she presents the product, packaging to camera.
  • Lifestyle — a natural moment holding or using it.
  • Detail — a close product view with her still in frame and recognizable.
  • Storytelling — an alternate angle with clean, caption-safe space.

The locks come first: her face, complexion, hair, body and wardrobe, plus the product's shape, colors and label placement, are locked from your real references — never invented, never slimmed, never swapped. You can add optional references, each with one job only: skin texture, body silhouette, a pose, or wardrobe. Pick up to four scenes (vanity, kitchen, studio, cafe, unboxing tabletop, testimonial, and more) or let the agent choose. Every shot is quality-checked — identity, product fidelity, hands and limbs — and delivered as an image-and-prompt pair, Shot 01 through Shot 04, so one product becomes four Pins, or the stills for a video Pin. It shines brightest in Product Spotlight mode.

Remember: the dashboard is your control panel — it does not browse, generate, or post by itself. The work happens in chat, from the Run in Chat prompt, and nothing is ever posted automatically.
Tap when your first full run is finished and its Pin package is in front of you.

Solo Agents — One Job at a Time

Lesson 5✓ Complete
Why This Matters

Sometimes you don't need the whole pipeline — you need one thing: fresh keywords for a pillar, one image fixed, copy for a Pin you already made. Solo forms let you hire a single agent for a single job, with the same rules, the same standing order, and the same approval gate — nothing watered down.

Follow Along
  • Open Solo Agent Forms — one form per agent, five in all, each one producing a clean, ready-to-copy prompt for that agent's job
  • Solo Scout: keywords, top boards, and seasonal trends for one pillar — your research run before a batch
  • Solo Pin Copy: hand it a finished Pin and it returns the full copy package — and it carries the standing order on its own form: a Pin is not done until it has a keyword title, a board, and a destination link
  • The solo Story and solo Video forms carry the Product Presentation choice too — one choice per run, None included, on every form where it belongs
  • Solo jobs land in the same Queue, newest first, and run the same way: Run in Chat, copy, paste, approve
Your Turn — Build It
  • Run a solo Scout job on the pillar you post most — save the keywords it brings back
  • Run a solo Pin Copy job on a Pin you already have — compare the new copy package to what is live now
  • Mark each job's status in the Queue as it moves: queued → working → done
Remember: solo does not mean sloppy. One agent, one job — same rules, same lock, same gate.
Tap when one solo Scout job and one solo Pin Copy job are done.

The 10-a-Day, 30-Day Scheduling System

Lesson 6The money system✓ Complete
Why This Matters

Random posting starves. A schedule feeds. Ten Pins a day, scheduled 30 days ahead in Pinterest's own scheduler, means your account works every single day — including the days you rest — and every Pin has a month to find its people. This rhythm is the difference between a hobby board and a sales channel.

Follow Along
  • The math: 10 Pins a day × 30 days = 300 Pins a month, built in weekly batches — about 70 to 75 at a time, never 300 in a panic
  • Scheduling happens in Pinterest's own scheduler — with Muse connected, Muse can fill it with you watching. Scheduled Pins sit in your Scheduled list until their day comes; scheduling is not posting
  • Two scheduler facts to respect: times land only on the hour and the half hour, and the calendar reaches about 30 days out — the last days unlock as the window rolls
  • Feed the batches from what you already have first — your photos, your art, your past posts, reissued as fresh Pins with keyword titles and real links. Create new only when a slot truly has nothing
  • Your Scout works 45 days ahead of the season, so holiday content is scheduled and warming up long before the holiday arrives
Your Turn — Build It
  • Map your first 7 days: 10 slots a day across your pillars — affirmations, looks, word art, decor, tips, spotlight
  • Fill the week from your existing content first; list only the real gaps as "create"
  • Schedule the whole week in Pinterest's scheduler, on :00 and :30 times, and confirm the count in your Scheduled list
Remember: consistency beats intensity. Ten a day, every day, a month ahead — that is the system. One heroic weekend is not.
Tap when your first 7 days — 70 Pins — are scheduled and counted.
7

Clicks & Sales — Links, Winners, and the Weekly Scoreboard

Lesson 7✓ Complete
Why This Matters

Saves are cute. Clicks pay. A beautiful Pin with no destination link is a dead end — pretty, and profit-free. Every Pin in this system carries a link to a product page, a shop, an interactive page, or a community — and every week you read one number, outbound clicks, to decide what gets made next. That scoreboard is how a farm becomes a business.

Follow Along
  • The standing order, one more time, because it is the whole game: a Pin is not done until it has a keyword title, a board, and a destination link
  • Product Spotlight Pins show the money path at its best: the Pin invites a tap, the destination is an interactive page people play with first — then they join or buy. Play first, purchase second
  • In Analytics, sort by outbound clicks, not impressions. Your proven winners get recreated — fresh versions of the concepts that already earned clicks. The Pins nobody clicks get retired, not repeated
  • Check every destination link like money depends on it — because it does. A broken link on a live Pin cannot be edited; get it right before the Pin ever goes out
  • No price, discount, or sale claim in any Pin copy unless a live price was actually verified — prices live on the product page
Your Turn — Build It
  • Open Analytics and name your top 3 Pins by outbound clicks — those are your winner concepts
  • Queue one recreation of each winner — same concept, fresh Pin, keyword title, working link
  • Put a weekly scoreboard check on your calendar: outbound clicks this week vs. last — one number, every week, no skipping
Remember: you didn't build a posting habit — you built a click system. Feed the winners, retire the rest, and let the scoreboard make the decisions.
Tap when your winners are named, their recreations are queued, and your weekly check is on the calendar.

PINTEREST FARM - THE ADAPTED BUILD PROMPT (Student Edition)
Built from THE AGENT FARM BUILD PROMPT (the master farm prompt),
adapted for Pinterest the same way the Facebook and Instagram farms were adapted.
Keep the bones. Change only what Pinterest genuinely changes.
================================================================================
Use this prompt to build / rebuild the dashboard. Where it conflicts with any
default, THIS prompt wins.

THE DASHBOARD
Build a working PINTEREST FARM dashboard - a real tool I can use, not a
document, not a lesson, not an explanation of one. Build it as ONE
self-contained interactive page (HTML) that runs when opened. If you cannot
make a file, output the complete code for that page so I can save and open it.
Do NOT stop at a plan or an outline - build the finished, working dashboard.

WHAT THIS FARM IS
One dashboard holds my Pin Intake Form, a Character Vault for my locked
characters, solo forms for each of my 5 agents, and a Queue for my runs. The
farm's job is to produce PINTEREST PINS for my Pinterest account @[YOUR HANDLE - replace this with your own Pinterest handle before you build] and my
digital products: [LIST YOUR OWN PRODUCTS HERE - your prompt packs, your art, your planners, your shop items]. After I submit a run, my AI runs the farm WITH me in
chat - Pinterest scout, locked character, story, image or video pin, pin
copy - and brings the finished pin package back for my approval. NOTHING is
ever posted automatically. Say that plainly inside the dashboard: the
dashboard itself is my control panel (intake, vault, queue); it does not
browse Pinterest, generate images, make videos, or post pins by itself - the
work happens in chat from the Run in Chat prompts.

DESIGN
Clean, pretty, phone-first. The dashboard lives in a Pinterest world: deep
Pinterest red and burgundy, pearl white glass panels, chrome silver accents,
a slow animated red ambient background with faint floating pin shapes, and
small butterfly touches. A full-screen PORTAL ENTRANCE opens first: the
portal-intro.mp4 red spiral tunnel video (kept in the same folder) plays
full-bleed behind the title PINTEREST FARM in huge glowing white letters,
the tagline "Where Pins Turn Into Sales", and one pearl-white Enter the
Farm button; tapping it zooms and fades through the portal into the
dashboard. The gate shows on every fresh load (it is never suppressed by
localStorage) and the page stays fully usable if the video file is missing.
Cards carry real 3D depth - layered shadows, glassy panels, and a gentle
pointer tilt on desktop. Big readable text, big tap-friendly buttons.
Everything that relates to video or character references is 9:16 vertical.
START EMPTY: no test characters, no test runs, no sample rows anywhere.
I add my real characters and runs myself. The dashboard saves in the
browser with localStorage key pinterestFarmV1.

PAGES THE DASHBOARD MUST HAVE
HOME: explains the farm, the agents (the 5 run agents plus the Pinterest
Setup Agent), the 7 modes, the Product Presentation rule, the Pin standing
order, and how Run in Chat works. The Pinterest Setup Agent's interactive
7-step Start From Zero checklist lives on Home; its checkmarks save inside
the same pinterestFarmV1 store under the setupProgress key.
PIN INTAKE FORM: the full farm run form (fields below, in order).
CHARACTER VAULT: locked characters (Character A, B, C...), each reusable as
that same character.
SOLO AGENT FORMS: one form per agent, so I can run a single agent job by
itself.
QUEUE: every full run and solo job in one list, newest first, with a manual
status I can set: queued, working, or done. The status button only changes
the label - it never executes anything. Execution happens in chat.
RUN IN CHAT: the card a Queue run opens into (see RUN IN CHAT below).

PIN INTAKE FORM - fields in this exact order:
1. Locked character picker (or "create a new character")
2. Mode (the 7 modes below)
3. PRODUCT PRESENTATION - right after Mode, one single choice: None
   (product not presented), Wearing the product, Holding the product, Using
   the product, Showing it close to camera, Sitting beside her in frame,
   Unboxing / opening it, Holding it up by her face, or Holding in some
   scenes and wearing in others. Wearing and holding never mix in the same
   shot. Every form category also offers a None option.
4. Topic
5. Pin format / video length (for example: a still pin, an idea pin, or a
   30 second video pin)
6. Product name and product link, when there is one
7. Reference link, when there is one (a pin, a board, or a video to learn from)
8. Notes
9. Destination link for this Pin (where the Pin sends people when they tap
   it - a product page, an interactive page, a shop, or a community page)
Save that one Product Presentation choice on the run. Show it on the Queue
card and on the Run in Chat card, with its exact prompt sentence (from THE
SCENE PROMPT SHAPE below). Show the destination link on the Queue card and
the Run in Chat card too, and bake it into the copied prompt.

CHARACTER VAULT - one record per locked character:
- Character letter and name (for example "Character A - Rosa"), plus a voice
  description.
- SLOT 1 - HEADSHOT: one photo, 9:16.
- SLOT 2 - BODY REFERENCE SHEET: ONE single 9:16 photo - not separate
  photos - with front, three-quarter, left profile, and right side views of
  the same person, same outfit, same background, all on that one photo.
- A character is NOT locked until BOTH slots are filled. Say so on the card.
- I can upload a character I already have: headshot for Slot 1, single 9:16
  body reference sheet for Slot 2. My uploads are accepted as JPG or PNG, as
  I saved them. Never force my uploads to PNG. If I upload a webp, reject it
  with a clear message: "JPG or PNG please."
- My characters are ultra realistic, with real skin texture and real hair
  texture. Every character is African American unless I named a specific
  existing character. Plus-size stays plus-size, never slimmed down.
- Within one video, identity, clothing, location, and lighting stay frozen
  from the reference photos.

THE 5 AGENTS - in this order:
AGENT 1 - PINTEREST SCOUT: scouts Pinterest itself in guest view (no
sign-in) for what is winning in the chosen mode (content pillar): the search
terms and keywords people are using, the top boards in the pillar, and the
seasonal trends - worked 45 days ahead of the season. When the run needs a
character look, it also picks the outfit or product reference on Pinterest,
especially products with an Amazon link, and copies its picture. Does not
check price or availability at this step.
AGENT 2 - IMAGE AGENT: creates the person and avatar images in chat -
character references, the outfit photo (my locked character wearing the
exact Pinterest outfit), and the outfit reference sheet. All ultra
realistic, real skin and hair texture, all PNG, all 9:16.
SCENE SET WORKFLOW (runs inside AGENT 2 - IMAGE AGENT, when a run's
Scene Set is on): transform ONE person reference plus ONE product
reference into FOUR distinct, premium, believable creator-style images,
each on its own independent canvas - stills ready to post as four
separate Pins or to carry into a video Pin. The four shots are planned
as one set: 1) HERO - the creator presents the product, recognizable
packaging facing camera. 2) LIFESTYLE - a natural moment holding or
using the product. 3) DETAIL - a close product view with the same
creator still visible and recognizable where framing permits.
4) STORYTELLING - an alternate action or angle with unobtrusive
caption-safe negative space.
1. CONFIRM AND INSPECT REFERENCES. Require an actual person image and
an actual product image; identify which upload serves each role and
request only a missing or unusable one - never invent a replacement
face or product. Build a compact identity checklist from visible
details (facial features, complexion, hairstyle, body proportions,
distinguishing traits, reference wardrobe) and a product checklist
(category, silhouette, geometry, packaging structure, colors, label
placement, plausible scale). Do not invent label copy, certifications,
features, testimonials, or performance claims; ask for a clearer label
reference when exact unreadable copy is essential.
2. OPTIONAL REFERENCES, ONE JOB EACH. Accept (never require) a skin
close-up (microtexture and finish only - not another person's
complexion or marks), a body/full-body reference (silhouette, build,
proportions, posture, fit - never its face), a pose reference, and
wardrobe images. Preserve the primary person's complexion, facial
features and existing body proportions unless I explicitly choose a
body reference or describe a change - never automatically slim,
enlarge, lengthen, or idealize. Ask one focused question only if
conflicting references make identity or body unclear.
3. REALISM STANDARD. Believable photographic realism: subtle pores at
viewing distance, fine facial hairs when visible, gentle tonal
variation, natural lip texture, softly defined under-eye detail,
knuckle creases, realistic nail beds, physically coherent highlights.
Preserve visible freckles, scars, stretch marks, or other defining
details the references support; do not invent or exaggerate
imperfections. Show natural soft-tissue compression, weight
distribution, joint placement, fabric tension, and contact shadows.
Avoid airbrushed waxy skin, doll-like faces, plastic highlights,
exaggerated curves, repeated hair strands, fake HDR, warped anatomy,
and excessive sharpening. Make lighting and camera choices concrete:
light direction, softness, color, shadow behavior, camera height,
perspective, focal-length look (plausible 35-50mm lifestyle or 70-85mm
portrait), depth of field, focus priority. Keep product labels and
facial detail legible rather than using extreme blur.
4. DIRECTION AND SCENES. All creative controls are optional - never
block production to ask about unspecified preferences. Defaults:
natural creator lifestyle setting appropriate to product use;
reference outfit and appearance; soft believable daylight; the farm's
Pin format (tall 2:3, or 9:16 when the set feeds a video Pin); natural,
plausible, commercially polished mood, pose, camera and props. Scene
choices: home / living room, kitchen / meal prep, bathroom / skincare
vanity, bedroom / getting ready, home office / desk, professional
studio, cafe / coffee shop, retail / shopping aisle, gym / fitness,
outdoor park / garden, city street / walking, travel / hotel /
airport, unboxing / tabletop, product demonstration / hands-on,
testimonial / talking-to-camera, beauty / fashion editorial - plus
custom locations. Preserve my direction text verbatim; do not read
direction text as a request for visible lettering unless I explicitly
ask for lettering. Scene allocation: no scene picked - choose one
believable environment and vary the four compositions; one scene -
four distinct compositions in it; two scenes - two images each; three
scenes - each once, repeat the first; four scenes - one each; more
than four - the first four in my order, and say briefly which were
used. Never merge scenes into one image or generate extra images.
5. GENERATE INDIVIDUALLY. Supply BOTH original references on every
generation. Exactly one final composition per generation, four final
outputs total - never a grid, collage, contact sheet, split screen, or
multiple scenes on one canvas. Every generation prompt carries the
identity checklist, the product checklist, the shot's scene and
interaction, applicable exact instructions, format, lighting, and the
single-canvas constraint. Keep reference labels where visible; no
added logos, decorative text, captions, watermarks, fabricated
packaging, or unrelated products. Backgrounds stay opaque unless I ask
for transparency. If generation is unavailable, deliver four numbered
copy-ready prompts instead - each fully standalone (both references,
locks, scene, composition, interaction, lighting, styling, format,
directions, constraints - never "same as above") - and say plainly
they are prompts. If generation fails partway, name the incomplete
outputs and offer prompts for those without claiming a complete set.
6. INSPECT AND DELIVER. Check every output: recognizable person,
consistent across the four; product shape, color, structure, label
fidelity, plausible size; natural hands, limbs, grip, contact points,
shadows, perspective; distinct compositions and selected-scene
coverage; correct format; balanced crop, clear product focus, caption
room, realistic texture, commercial polish. Regenerate visibly failing
shots when supported; never claim exact identity or label accuracy
that cannot be confirmed, and disclose material unresolved issues
briefly. Deliver four image-and-prompt pairs in shot order - Image 01
+ Prompt 01 through Image 04 + Prompt 04 - each prompt the FINAL
version after any refinement, standing alone: every reference's role,
identity and complexion locks, body and skin-texture instructions,
wardrobe, scene and action, shot size, camera angle and lens look,
light direction and shadow quality, environmental and material
textures, product scale, label and grip, composition and caption-safe
space, format, applicable exact instructions, and negative
constraints. Describe only features actually observed in the
references. Later revisions update the one named image and preserve
the set's locks rather than recreating all four. Never represent
generated scenes as documentary evidence of a real endorsement; add no

Watch Out For This

Real snags from a real build — know them now and save yourself the hour they cost.

Before you hit a wall

Every one of these came up while this exact system was being built and run on a live account. Here's what to watch for — and the way through each one.

  • Hunting for a connector that doesn't exist: there is no Pinterest connector in Muse's Settings, so don't burn an hour looking for one. The browser is the connection — Lesson 1 is the whole setup.
  • Passwords and codes: they go only into Pinterest's login page inside the browser panel. If Muse ever seems to need one in chat, stop — that's not how this works, ever.
  • Scheduler times: Pinterest's scheduler only offers on-the-hour and half-hour times. There is no 7:15. Plan your day on :00s and :30s and you'll never fight the dropdown.
  • The 30-day wall: the scheduler only reaches about 30 days out. Your last day or two unlocks as the window rolls forward — check back and slot them in when they open instead of forcing them early.
  • A wrong link on a scheduled Pin: a scheduled Pin's link cannot be edited. The fix is to delete that one scheduled Pin and re-schedule it with the right link — so read every destination link twice before you schedule.
  • A wrong link on a live Pin: a published Pin's link is locked for good. If the link is dead, remake the Pin with a working link and retire the old one. Winners with working links stay untouched.
  • Webp in the Vault: the Character Vault takes JPG or PNG only. If your photo is a webp, you'll get "JPG or PNG please" — convert it and upload again.
  • Describing her in a scene prompt: never do it. Describing the character overrides her reference photos and creates a different girl. Scene prompts carry action only — the references are the lock.

The Rules That Protect Your Pinterest

The rules that never change, no matter how fast your farm runs.
Rule 1

The standing order

A Pin is not done until it has a keyword title, a board, and a destination link. No exceptions, no "post it now, link it later."

Rule 2

Your approval is the gate

Nothing is posted automatically, ever. The farm brings finished packages to chat; posting always needs your separate yes.

Rule 3

Existing content first

Feed the farm what you already have — photos, art, past posts — reissued fresh. Create new only when a slot truly has nothing.

Rule 4

Recreate winners, retire losers

Your proven Pins earn fresh recreations. Pins nobody clicks get retired, not repeated. The scoreboard decides.

Rule 5

No unverified price claims

No price, discount, or sale claim in any Pin copy unless a live price was actually verified. Prices live on the product page.

Rule 6

The lock is the lock

Scene prompts carry action only — never a description of her. And a product gets one presentation choice per run — wearing and holding never mix in the same shot.
